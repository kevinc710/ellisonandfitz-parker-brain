# Creative Memory: Ellison Fluid Pant

This document serves as the persistent creative intelligence for the **Ellison Fluid Pant**. It tracks what works, what doesn't, and the evolving prompt philosophy for generating new ad assets.

As new data comes in from Meta, this file should be updated to reflect the latest learnings.

## 1. The Core Creative Philosophy

The biggest lesson from the August 2026 creative batch: **Stop thinking in terms of "ads." Think in terms of "micro-demonstrations."**

Every winning shot should answer exactly **one** question:
- How do the pleats move?
- How does the fabric catch light?
- What makes the silhouette different?
- What happens when someone walks?
- What does the texture feel like?
- Why would someone look twice?

If a clip answers more than one question, it is trying to do too much. If it answers one question exceptionally well, it becomes a reusable building block that the editor (Daniela) can combine into dozens of different ads.

### The Prompting Shift (Cinematography over Description)

**Old way (Do not use):**
> "Man wearing pants walking through a city."

**New way (Use this):**
> "Generate a handheld, waist-height product discovery shot where the camera unexpectedly notices the Ellison & Fitz Fluid Pant as the model walks through a clean concrete environment. The pants occupy at least 60% of the frame. Begin with movement already in progress—no static pose. Emphasize the exaggerated wide-leg silhouette, pleated texture, and natural drape. The camera should subtly follow the pants as if the operator is curious about them. Natural light, premium but native feel, no text, no voiceover, 6–8 seconds, 9:16."

**Key principles for AI video generation (Higgsfield/Kling):**
1. Direct camera behavior, not just the scene.
2. Specify the exact angle (e.g., "knee height looking slightly upward").
3. Define the interaction (e.g., "hands run across the fabric").
4. Keep the environment secondary to the product.

## 3. The Next Generation (Phase 2 Concepts)

This is the active testing ground. These concepts represent the new "micro-demonstration" philosophy and are the highest priority for new generation.

### FITZ-CR-0061: Visual Puzzle
- **Hook:** Visual Puzzle
- **Angle:** Pleat Discovery
- **Concept:** Open on an extreme macro of the pleated fabric filling the frame. The viewer shouldn't immediately recognize it's a pair of pants. As the camera slowly pulls back, the waistband and silhouette are revealed while the fabric catches directional light. No face. No text. The objective is to make the viewer pause to figure out what they're seeing before realizing it's the Fluid Pant.

### FITZ-CR-0062: Passing Stranger
- **Hook:** Discovery
- **Angle:** Passing Stranger
- **Concept:** The camera behaves like someone walking through the city who unexpectedly notices the pants. Begin focused on the environment, then naturally pan and track the model as they pass. The reveal should feel accidental rather than staged. The pants become the reason the camera changes direction.

### FITZ-CR-0063: Architecture
- **Hook:** Low Angle
- **Angle:** Architecture
- **Concept:** Film from knee height looking slightly upward while the model walks through clean architectural surroundings. The low angle exaggerates the wide-leg silhouette and the drape. The pants should dominate the frame.

### FITZ-CR-0064: First Person
- **Hook:** POV
- **Angle:** First Person
- **Concept:** Chest-height first-person view looking down while walking naturally. The viewer experiences the movement as if they're wearing the pants themselves. Focus on the rhythm of the pleats and how the fabric responds to each step.

### FITZ-CR-0065: Hands
- **Hook:** Texture
- **Angle:** Hands
- **Concept:** A library of tactile product shots. Hands run across the fabric, pinch the pleats, stretch the material, and let it fall naturally. Minimal environment. Clean directional lighting. Build a reusable asset library rather than a finished advertisement.

### FITZ-CR-0066: Stair Climb
- **Hook:** Stair Climb
- **Angle:** Movement
- **Concept:** The model climbs concrete stairs from a low camera angle. Each step should emphasize the silhouette and the movement of the pleats. The environment remains secondary to the product.

### FITZ-CR-0067: Escalator
- **Hook:** Escalator
- **Angle:** Travel
- **Concept:** The model rides an escalator while the camera stays low and close. The repeating motion naturally showcases the drape and movement of the pants without feeling staged.

### FITZ-CR-0068: Micro Interaction
- **Hook:** Pocket Detail
- **Angle:** Micro Interaction
- **Concept:** A close-up of the hand sliding into the pocket, adjusting naturally, then walking away. The movement creates subtle shifts in the pleats and silhouette. The interaction should feel effortless rather than demonstrative.

### FITZ-CR-0069: Fabric Motion
- **Hook:** Wind
- **Angle:** Fabric Motion
- **Concept:** The model walks through a lightly breezy environment where the fabric catches the wind. The movement should feel organic and showcase the lightweight drape of the pants.

### FITZ-CR-0070: Editorial
- **Hook:** Mirror
- **Angle:** Editorial
- **Concept:** The model walks past a full-length mirror. The reflection reveals the silhouette from a second perspective without requiring additional cuts. The focus remains on the movement of the pants.

## 7. The 5 Editing Frameworks

> Each framework is a reusable 4-slot sequence. Swap GOLD clips into each slot to generate dozens of ads. Build one master edit per framework before creating any variations.

### Framework 1 — Product Discovery

**Core idea:** Reveal the product progressively. Start so close the viewer cannot identify it, then pull back.
**Duration:** 7–9s | **Hook type:** Visual Puzzle

| Slot | Timing | Description | Best GOLD Clips |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slot 1 — Macro Texture | 0–2s | Extreme close-up of pleated fabric. Viewer cannot identify what they are seeing. | IMG_0491, IMG_0487, IMG_0519 |
| Slot 2 — Pull Back | 2–4s | Camera pulls back or cuts to reveal the full garment. The "aha" moment. | IMG_0686, IMG_0637, IMG_0489 |
| Slot 3 — Walking | 4–7s | Model walks, showing the garment in motion. Fabric flow is visible. | IMG_0543, IMG_0556, IMG_0626 |
| Slot 4 — Wide Silhouette | 7–9s | Final frame: wide-leg silhouette, clean background. | IMG_0684, IMG_0679, IMG_0686 |

**Variations:** (A) Start even tighter — single pleat line only | (B) Start on waistband instead of pleats | (C) Start on color shift | (D) Remove Slot 4, end on walking | (E) Speed ramp on Slot 3

## 8. Hook Pattern Research Playbook

> **For the VA:** Collect 100 ads from Meta Ad Library, TikTok, and Instagram Reels. Watch **only the first second** of each. Log the hook pattern. Do not watch the full ad. This becomes the editing playbook.

### Hook Pattern Categories

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| **MACRO** | Extreme close-up of fabric/texture. Viewer cannot identify the product immediately. |
| **POV** | First-person perspective. Looking down at the product or walking. |
| **STATIC** | Model or product is completely still in the first frame. |
| **MOVEMENT** | Model is already in motion when the ad starts. Walking, turning, striding. |
| **ZOOM** | Camera zooms in or out on the product in the first second. |
| **ARCHITECTURE** | First frame is an environment (concrete, room, street) with no model visible. |
| **COLOR** | First frame is dominated by a single bold color or color contrast. |
| **SILHOUETTE** | Model is backlit or in shadow — shape visible but no detail. |
| **HAND/TOUCH** | A hand is touching, adjusting, or interacting with the product. |
| **JUMP CUT** | Rapid cut within the first second — two or more frames in under 1 second. |
| **FREEZE** | Video starts frozen on a single frame, then begins moving. |
| **OTHER** | Does not fit any category above. Describe in notes. |

### What to Look For

After collecting 100 hooks, categorize them and look for patterns like:

```
MACRO → Jump Cut → Walking
POV → Turn → Macro
Static → Zoom → Movement
```

These sequences become the E&F editing playbook — grounded in what is actually winning in the market, not what looks good in theory.

## 11. Editor Instructions (Daniela)

### The Most Important Rule

> Do not try to make "good videos." Try to isolate **winning visual moments**.
>
> Every ad should revolve around **one memorable shot**. The other shots only exist to support that hero moment. That is exactly what the original Fluid Pant winner did — it had one or two unforgettable visual moments that carried the entire creative.

### Order of Operations

1. **Grade the GOLD clips** — Watch all 50 and score them on the 4 scroll-stop questions in the workbook. Note where you disagree with the AI scores.
2. **Build one master edit per framework** — 5 edits total. Not 20 different ads.
3. **Ask of each master:** Does this have one unforgettable visual moment? If yes, it is your master. If no, swap clips until you find the hero moment.
4. **Generate 5 variations per framework** — Different opening, first frame, ending, pacing, text overlay.
5. **Log every result** in the Performance Feedback Loop tab of the workbook.
6. **Update the Heatmap** after each batch with real win rates.

### Editing Rules

| Rule | Instruction |
|---|---|
| Sound off test | Every ad must make sense with no sound. The visual tells the entire story. |
| One idea only | Texture OR movement OR silhouette. Never combine in one 7-second ad. |
| First frame is everything | If the first frame does not stop the scroll, nothing else matters. |
| No text overlays | Unless explicitly specified in the brief. |
| No logo until end frame | If included at all. |
| Default transition | Hard cut. Only use speed ramps or freeze frames when explicitly noted. |
