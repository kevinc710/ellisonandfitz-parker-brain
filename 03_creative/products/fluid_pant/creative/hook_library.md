# Fluid Pant Hook Library

## Rule

A hook is not a complete creative strategy. It is the first moment that earns the right to show product proof. Each hook must create curiosity **and remain relevant to a visible Fluid Pant differentiator** in the first 0.5 to 1.5 seconds.

## Benchmark question

Use this as the primary first-second filter:

> **Which hook immediately communicates that these are not ordinary pants, while making me curious enough to keep watching?**

A hook can be visually strange without being useful. If it creates curiosity but does not reinforce texture, drape, silhouette, light interaction, construction, or another Fluid Pant differentiator, it fails the benchmark.

## Evidence semantics

- **PERFORMANCE-PROVEN** = completed Fluid Pant ad testing supports relative performance.
- **PRODUCT-PROVEN** = the visual reliably demonstrates a real product property, but its relative hook performance may be unknown.
- **STRONG SIGNAL** = product-specific evidence or pre-test analysis makes the hook a priority, but it is not yet a proven winner.
- **HYPOTHESIS** = plausible and testable; not established.

Do not convert an approved hook framework into a performance claim.

## Hook candidates

| Hook | Evidence status | Product proof | Use case | What is not yet known |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme Pleat Macro | **STRONG SIGNAL** | Pleated micro-stripe texture and delayed recognition | Product Discovery | Whether it is highest-performing versus matched alternatives |
| Authentic Color Shift | **STRONG SIGNAL** | Directional light reveals real tonal variation and pleats | Product Discovery, Texture | Which light treatment/crop is strongest and whether it wins at equal spend |
| Low-Angle Stride | **STRONG SIGNAL** | Wide straight-leg silhouette and drape under movement | Movement | Whether it beats other hook types at equal spend |
| Pocket Interaction | **STRONG SIGNAL** | Natural tactile gesture and product detail | Texture, POV | Relative performance versus stride or macro control |
| Waistband Reveal | **HYPOTHESIS** | Drawstring and pleat construction | Texture, product detail | Whether it works as a first frame or only in the body edit |
| Mirror | **HYPOTHESIS** | Full look plus wearer perspective | POV, styling | Whether mirror competes with product focus |
| Impossible Crop | **HYPOTHESIS** | Delayed recognition and visual puzzle | Product Discovery | Whether abstraction creates curiosity without losing product relevance |
| Walking | **PRODUCT-PROVEN** | Drape, step, and silhouette | Support shot and movement body edit | Whether it is stronger than alternatives as an opening hook |
| Hand Brush / Fabric Discovery | **STRONG SIGNAL** | Pleat definition and tactile detail | Texture | Whether hand interaction outperforms pocket or macro at equal spend |
| Hem in Motion | **HYPOTHESIS** | Lower-leg drape and pleat movement | Movement | Whether the crop communicates enough product novelty |
| Side Silhouette | **STRONG SIGNAL** | Relaxed wide straight-leg proportion | Silhouette, Movement | Whether full-length readability beats tighter curiosity crops |

## Hook tournament protocol

1. Use one body edit, one audio condition, and one offer condition.
2. Change only the first 0.5 to 1.5 seconds.
3. Keep product/colorway, audience, placement, and end frame comparable.
4. Track Thumb Stop Rate first, then 3-second Hold Rate.
5. Only after hook performance is understood, assess CTR, CPP, and conversion outcomes.
6. Do not call the predicted top hook a winner before the matched test is complete.
7. Log results in [Testing History](../research/testing_history.md).

## Initial visual direction

The hook should make the viewer ask one product-relevant question instantly:

- What is that texture?
- Why does the fabric move like that?
- Why does the silhouette look different?
- What changes when the light hits it?
- What are those pants?
- Why would I look twice at them?

If the first frame could describe any ordinary pant, it is not a Fluid Pant hook.

## Do not do

- Open on a creator's face before product evidence.
- Use a generic lifestyle establishing shot.
- Use an unsupported product claim as a hook.
- Use an abstract visual puzzle that has no recognizable connection to the product.
- Change hook, copy, audio, offer, body edit, and audience at once.
- Use a non-Fluid Pant result as a Fluid Pant ranking.
