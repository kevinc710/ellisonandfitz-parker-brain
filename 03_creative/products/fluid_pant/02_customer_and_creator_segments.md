# Fluid Pant Customer and Creator Segments

## Purpose

This file gives the team a consistent way to tailor Fluid Pant creative and outreach without turning broad demographic assumptions into facts. Segments are **working hypotheses**. A creator or customer should be selected because their visible style, audience, and content context fit the story, not because of an assumed age, income, or identity.

## Core buyer tension

The Fluid Pant is for someone who wants comfort and ease **without disappearing into generic clothing**. The product earns attention through visible movement, pleated texture, drape, color shift, and a silhouette that looks different from an ordinary trouser.

## Core buyer profile

| Dimension | Working profile |
|---|---|
| Style mindset | Wants to express personal taste rather than dress like everyone else |
| Purchase trigger | Notices an unusual silhouette, fabric movement, texture, color shift, or styling possibility |
| Desired outcome | Feels comfortable, considered, and distinct in everyday life |
| Best proof | Visual demonstration, credible personal styling, and a specific first-person observation |
| Weak proof | Generic comfort copy, flat product description, or a long lifestyle monologue before the pant appears |

## Working customer and creator archetypes

These archetypes guide concept development. They are not fixed demographic targets.

### 1. The Creative Individualist

**Visible context:** Artist, musician, designer, photographer, stylist, or any person with a distinctive personal aesthetic.

**Core tension:** "I want clothes that have personality without feeling like a costume."

**Best Fluid Pant proof:** A personal-style statement followed immediately by movement, texture, and a full silhouette.

**Strong creative contexts:** Rehearsal, studio, gallery, city walk, creative workspace, evening plans, or dressing for a show.

**Do not do:** Treat the creator's age, profession, or music taste as the entire concept. The pant still needs to appear in the first second.

### 2. The Movement-Led Everyday Dresser

**Visible context:** Travel, walking city routes, commuting, active days, errand-to-evening routines, and expressive but practical styling.

**Core tension:** "I need one piece that moves with me and still looks considered."

**Best Fluid Pant proof:** Walking, stepping, turning, pocket interaction, and a quick outfit change or day-to-night sequence.

**Strong creative contexts:** Airport or transit environment, escalator, concrete stairs, city street, café, or a casual night out.

**Do not do:** Make unsupported performance claims or turn the asset into a generic athleisure ad.

### 3. The Style-Forward Entrepreneur

**Visible context:** Founder, freelancer, creative operator, consultant, or self-directed professional with a visible personal brand.

**Core tension:** "I want to look deliberate without dressing rigidly."

**Best Fluid Pant proof:** Clean architecture, low-angle silhouette, a simple outfit formula, and a real workday-to-evening transition.

**Strong creative contexts:** Studio, workspace, city architecture, coffee meeting, travel day, or evening event.

**Do not do:** Make the pant a corporate uniform or lean on business success claims.

### 4. The Design-Curious Style Explorer

**Visible context:** Follows visual culture, notices materials and proportion, and responds to something unfamiliar or visually arresting.

**Core tension:** "I want the piece that makes someone look twice."

**Best Fluid Pant proof:** Macro pleats, color shift, texture pull, waistband reveal, and visual puzzle hooks.

**Strong creative contexts:** Minimal architecture, directional light, mirror, tactile close-up, and product discovery.

**Do not do:** Overexplain. Let the product create curiosity first.

## Creator-fit scorecard

| Criterion | What to look for | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Product-story fit | The creator can naturally place the pant in a believable setting | Reduces forced endorsements |
| On-camera delivery | Direct, specific, conversational, and credible | Supports native creator content |
| Visual execution | Can show a full look, movement, fabric, or styling with usable lighting | Makes the product the hero |
| Audience relevance | An audience likely to value distinctive, comfortable, expressive dressing | Increases affiliate fit |
| Reliability | Follows a simple prompt, communicates clearly, and meets an agreed deadline | Determines operational fit |
| Rights readiness | Open to clear terms if content later moves into paid media | Keeps affiliate and UGC paths separate |

## Personalization rule for VAs

Use the candidate's **visible context** to tailor the first sentence. Then move to a standard screening request. Do not use a person's age, location, job title, or personal history as the sole reason for outreach.

**Good:** "Your performance and creative background could make for an interesting Fluid Pant story around movement and personal style."

**Not good:** "We need an older musician to reach people your age."

## Evidence needed before changing this file

Do not add audience, avatar, or demographic claims from a single conversation. Add or revise a hypothesis only when it has a date, source, sample, and business implication, such as customer-survey results, Shopify cohort data, Meta audience performance, creator post results, or a documented research project.
